## Key Takeaways

- **A HELOC is a revolving line of credit** secured by your home. You draw what you need and pay interest only on what you use.
- **Keep your current mortgage rate.** Freddie Mac's survey showed early-2026 rates near 6%, below today's 7.18%. A HELOC leaves that first mortgage alone.

- **Tax rules:** Interest may be deductible only when funds are used to buy, build, or substantially improve the home, per the IRS.

## What Is a HELOC and How Does It Work?

A **home equity line of credit (HELOC)** is a second mortgage that works a bit like a credit card backed by your house. It usually has two phases:

- **Draw period** (often around 10 years): You can borrow, repay, and borrow again. Many HELOCs require interest-only payments during this time.
- **Repayment period** (often 10 to 20 years): You can't draw anymore, and payments include principal and interest.

**Equity** is your home's value minus what you owe. **Combined loan-to-value (CLTV)** is all the loans on the home divided by its value. Lenders use CLTV to set your line size.

## What Are HELOC Rates in November 2026?

Bankrate's national average HELOC rate was **7.49%** as of November 4, 2026, up 13 basis points from the prior week, with a survey range of 3.99% to 11.95%. Bankrate's average assumes a $30,000 line, a 700 credit score, and 80% CLTV.

Most HELOCs are priced as the **prime rate plus a margin**. The Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.75% to 4.00% on October 28, 2026, and the next meeting is December 8-9. If the Fed changes rates, HELOC rates typically follow.

**Payment example:** If you draw $40,000 at 7.49% during an interest-only draw period, your payment would be about **$250 per month**. Payments rise once repayment starts or if the rate goes up. This is an illustration, not a quote.

## HELOC or Cash-Out Refinance?

| | HELOC | Cash-out refinance |
|---|---|---|
| First mortgage | Stays in place | Replaced with a new, larger loan |
| Rate | Usually variable | Usually fixed |
| Best when | Your current rate is below today's rates | Your current rate is near or above today's rates |
| Access to funds | Draw as needed | Lump sum at closing |
| Costs | Often lower upfront | Full closing costs |

With the 30-year fixed at 7.18% in Freddie Mac's November 5 survey, many Hendersonville owners who bought or refinanced in recent years have a lower first-mortgage rate they'd rather keep. That's often where a HELOC fits.

## What Are Smart Ways to Use a HELOC?

- **Home improvements:** Porches, kitchens, roofs, and additions. These can add value and may qualify for the interest deduction.
- **Lake-home projects:** If you're on Old Hickory Lake, remember docks and shoreline work need U.S. Army Corps of Engineers permits. Our [Old Hickory Lake homes guide](/posts/old-hickory-lake-homes-hendersonville/) explains the rules.
- **Paying off high-rate debt:** This can lower your interest cost, but you're turning unsecured debt into debt secured by your home. Have a payoff plan.
- **Emergency backup:** Some owners open a line and leave it unused for peace of mind.

Use caution with everyday spending. Missed HELOC payments put your home at risk.

## Are HELOC Interest Payments Tax-Deductible?

The IRS allows a deduction for home equity interest only if the money is used to **buy, build, or substantially improve** the home that secures the loan, within the overall $750,000 mortgage debt limit for most filers. Interest on funds used for debt payoff or other expenses isn't deductible. Talk with a tax professional about your situation. This article isn't tax advice.

## How Do You Apply for a HELOC?

1. **Check your numbers:** current balance, estimated value, and credit score.
2. **Gather documents:** pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, a mortgage statement, and homeowners insurance.
3. **Compare offers:** look at the margin, any intro rate, annual fees, draw minimums, and early-closure fees.
4. **Complete the valuation:** some lenders use an appraisal, others an automated valuation.
5. **Close and draw:** most HELOCs on a primary residence include a three-day right to cancel before funds are available.
