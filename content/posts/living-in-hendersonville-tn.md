**Living in Hendersonville, TN means lake-town life about 18 miles northeast of Nashville, with deep frontier history and a busy community calendar. It's Sumner County's largest city, with 61,753 residents in the 2020 census. In September 2026, Hendersonville's median sale price was about $515,000, according to Redfin.**

If you're thinking about moving here, it helps to know the town as well as the market. Here's a quick look at Hendersonville's story, what's happening this November, and what buyers should plan for.

## Key Takeaways

- **Lake city near Nashville:** Hendersonville sits on Old Hickory Lake, about 18 miles from downtown Nashville.
- **Deep roots:** settlement began in the 1780s, and Rock Castle, finished in 1796, is now a state historic site.
- **Young as a city:** Hendersonville incorporated in 1969, when it had about 250 residents. It counted 61,753 people in 2020.
- **November events:** STUMPBERFEST on Nov. 7 and the Veterans Day Parade on Nov. 8.
- **Market snapshot:** a $514,755 median sale price in September 2026, up 3.5% from a year earlier, with about 65 days on market.

## How Did Hendersonville Get Its Start?

The area's story begins in the 1780s. Daniel Smith, a surveyor and Revolutionary War veteran, received a land grant here in 1784 and built **Rock Castle**, a stone home finished in 1796, the same year Tennessee became a state. Today Rock Castle is a state historic site that hosts tours and events, and it's one of the oldest homes in Middle Tennessee.

The community took its name from **William Henderson**, its first postmaster. During the Civil War, the **Monthaven** estate served as a Union field hospital. The house still stands today.

## How Did Hendersonville Grow So Fast?

For most of its history, Hendersonville was a small farming community. The creation of Old Hickory Lake in the 1950s changed that, drawing families who wanted water views close to Nashville. The city incorporated in 1969 with only about 250 residents. By the 2020 census, it had grown to 61,753, the largest city in Sumner County.

The lake is still the town's signature. Shoreline parks, marinas, and waterfront neighborhoods shape daily life. If lake living is your goal, read our guide to [buying Old Hickory Lake homes](/posts/old-hickory-lake-homes-hendersonville/).

## What Is There to Do in Hendersonville This November?

- **STUMPBERFEST** – Saturday, Nov. 7, 2026, at Half Batch Brewing. This fall festival is listed on the [Hendersonville Area Chamber of Commerce](https://www.hendersonvillechamber.com/) calendar.
- **Veterans Day Parade** – Sunday, Nov. 8, 2026, at 2 p.m. The route runs from Cherokee Drive to Executive Park Drive, and retired Col. Free See is grand marshal.
- **Fall at Rock Castle** – the historic site is a great place for an autumn walk and a lesson in local history.
- **Thanksgiving on the lake** – cooler days make the parks and greenways quieter, a good time to explore neighborhoods.

Check with organizers and the [City of Hendersonville](https://www.hvilletn.org/) before you go, since dates and times can change.
