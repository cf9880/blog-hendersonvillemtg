**What should you know before buying an Old Hickory Lake home in Hendersonville?** The shoreline is managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, so docks and shoreline work need Corps permits, and those permits don't transfer automatically when a home sells. Confirm what's permitted, budget for insurance and upkeep, and choose a loan that fits the property before you make an offer.

Hendersonville calls itself "The City by the Lake" for good reason. Here's a buyer's guide to Old Hickory Lake homes in Hendersonville, with a little history, the rules that surprise many buyers, and how financing works.

## Key takeaways

- Old Hickory Lake was created by the Corps' Old Hickory Lock and Dam, with dam closure in **June 1954**.
- The lake covers about **22,500 surface acres** with roughly **440 miles of shoreline** at normal pool (USACE).
- Private docks and shoreline use require a **Corps Shoreline Use Permit**. Permits are **not transferable** to a new owner.
- Lake homes can be financed with conventional, jumbo, FHA, or VA loans for a primary residence, or a DSCR loan for a rental.

## How did Old Hickory Lake shape Hendersonville?

Before the dam, Hendersonville was a small farming village along Highway 31E. The Rivers and Harbors Act of 1946 authorized Old Hickory Lock and Dam. Construction started in January 1952, dam closure came in June 1954, and the project reached full use in December 1957, according to the [U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Nashville District](https://www.lrn.usace.army.mil/Locations/Dams/Old-Hickory-Dam/).

The new lake created peninsulas and coves, and people noticed. The Hendersonville Chamber of Commerce notes that the lake drew musicians and industry executives. Sumner County's own website says country stars including Johnny Cash, June Carter Cash, and Roy Orbison settled along its banks. Hendersonville incorporated as a city in 1969 and grew fast after that.

## Fun facts about Old Hickory Lake

- The lake stretches **97.3 river miles** upstream to Cordell Hull Lock and Dam near Carthage (USACE).
- Water levels stay fairly steady, with normal pool at **445 feet** above sea level and a minimum of 442 feet. That's great news for dock owners.
- USACE lists **eight marinas, two Corps campgrounds, and 41 boat access sites** on the lake.
- **Historic Rock Castle**, built by Daniel Smith starting in 1784, sits on the lake's shore. When the lake filled in 1954, it covered much of the old land behind the house.

## Who controls the shoreline on Old Hickory Lake?

This is the biggest surprise for many buyers. On most of Old Hickory Lake, the land right at the water's edge is **public land managed by the Corps**, even when your lot looks like it runs to the water. The Corps' **Shoreline Management Plan** sets the rules for docks, mowing, paths, and clearing vegetation.

### What is a Shoreline Use Permit?

A Shoreline Use Permit is Corps permission to put a private floating dock or do approved work, like mowing, on Corps-managed shoreline next to your property. Rules cover dock size, design, and spacing, and not every shoreline section allows new docks.

### Does a dock permit transfer when a lake home sells?

No. Under the Old Hickory Lake Shoreline Management Plan, shoreline permits are **non-transferable**. The new owner applies for a new permit after closing. Before you buy, ask the seller for documentation from the Corps on the current permit and dock status. Then confirm with the Nashville District Old Hickory Lake resource manager's office.

## What to check before buying a Hendersonville lake home

- **Dock status:** Is there an existing permitted dock, and does that shoreline section allow one?
- **Flood zone and insurance:** Ask your insurance agent for quotes early. Your lender will need proof of coverage, and flood insurance may be required depending on the flood zone.
- **Slope and access:** Many lots slope steeply to the water. Check stairs, retaining walls, and drainage.
- **Septic or sewer:** Some older lake homes use septic. Get it inspected.
- **Appraisal:** Waterfront comps can be limited, so appraisals may take longer. Plan your timeline around that.

## How do you finance an Old Hickory Lake home?

Lake homes come in every price range, from older cottages in coves to large custom homes. Common options include:

- **Conventional loans:** As little as 3% down for qualified first-time buyers. 20% down avoids private mortgage insurance.
- **Jumbo loans:** For loan amounts above the conforming limit, which is common for larger waterfront homes. Mortgage News Daily's index had a 30-year jumbo at 7.65% on October 20, 2026.
- **FHA and VA loans:** Available for a primary residence that meets program and appraisal standards. See our [VA loan guide for Hendersonville](../va-loan-hendersonville-tn/).
- **DSCR loans:** For investors buying a lake rental. A DSCR loan qualifies mainly on the property's rental income compared with its payment, not your personal income. Check local short-term rental rules before you count on rental income.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I build a dock on Old Hickory Lake?

Possibly. Private floating docks need a U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Shoreline Use Permit, and only certain shoreline sections allow new docks. Check with the Corps before you buy.

### Do Old Hickory Lake dock permits transfer to a new owner?

No. Shoreline permits on Old Hickory Lake are non-transferable, so a buyer must apply for a new permit after closing.

### How big is Old Hickory Lake?

About 22,500 surface acres with roughly 440 miles of shoreline at normal pool, extending 97.3 river miles upstream to Cordell Hull Lock and Dam, per the Army Corps of Engineers.

### When was Old Hickory Lake created?

Construction of Old Hickory Lock and Dam began in January 1952, dam closure was completed in June 1954, and the project reached full use in December 1957.
