**Refinancing in Hendersonville TN is worth it when the savings or benefits outweigh the costs within the time you plan to keep the loan. With 30-year rates near 7.3% in mid-November 2026, a lower rate alone helps mainly recent buyers who locked higher. Other good reasons include removing mortgage insurance, leaving an ARM, or consolidating debt.**

Many Sumner County homeowners are asking the same question as the year winds down: should I refinance now, or wait? The honest answer depends on your current loan, your goals, and how long you'll stay in your home. Here's a plain-English guide from CFC Mortgage — Hendersonville.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rates rose this week.** Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed index was 7.30% on November 10, 2026, after a hotter-than-expected inflation report.
- **A year ago, rates were lower.** Freddie Mac's 30-year average was 6.22% a year before its November 5, 2026 reading of 7.18%.
- **Run the break-even math:** total closing costs divided by monthly savings.
- **Tennessee adds a mortgage indebtedness tax** of $0.115 per $100 of recorded debt, with the first $2,000 exempt, per the Tennessee Department of Revenue.
- **A refinance isn't only about rate.** Dropping PMI, changing loan terms, or tapping equity can also make sense.

## Is Now a Good Time to Refinance in Hendersonville?

For many homeowners who bought or refinanced in 2020 and 2021, today's rates are well above what they have. Refinancing just for a lower rate usually won't help them.

It's a different story if you:

- **Bought in the past year at a higher rate.** Mortgage News Daily's 30-year index has ranged from 5.99% to 7.61% over the past 52 weeks. If you locked near the top, even a small drop could pay off.
- **Have an FHA loan with mortgage insurance** and enough equity to move to a conventional loan.
- **Have an adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM)** that's about to reset and want a steady payment.
- **Need cash** for a major project or to pay off higher-rate debt.

## How Do I Calculate a Refinance Break-Even Point?

Your **break-even point** is how long it takes for monthly savings to cover your closing costs.

**Break-even (months) = total refinance costs / monthly savings**

Here's a simple example. It's an illustration only, not a quote or approval:

| Item | Example |
|---|---|
| Loan balance | $300,000 |
| Current principal and interest at 7.75% | About $2,149/month |
| New principal and interest at 7.18% | About $2,032/month |
| Monthly savings | About $117 |
| Example closing costs | $5,500 |
| **Break-even** | **About 47 months** |

If you plan to stay in your Hendersonville home longer than about four years in this example, the refinance could make sense. If you might move sooner, it probably won't. Restarting a 30-year term also adds years of payments, so compare total interest too.

## What Does It Cost to Refinance in Tennessee?

Refinance costs often include lender fees, an appraisal, title insurance, a settlement fee, and recording charges. Tennessee also has a state **indebtedness tax** on recorded mortgages.

According to the [Tennessee Department of Revenue](https://www.tn.gov/revenue/taxes/local-taxes/recordation-taxes/due-date-tax-rate.html), the mortgage tax is **$0.115 per $100 of indebtedness**, and the first $2,000 is exempt. On a $300,000 recorded loan, that's about $342.70. The department's Recordation Tax Manual says that when debt increases beyond a previously stated maximum, tax is due on the increase. How it applies to your refinance depends on how the new deed of trust is recorded, so check the figure on your Loan Estimate.

## What Are Good Reasons to Refinance Besides a Lower Rate?

### Remove Mortgage Insurance

**Private mortgage insurance (PMI)** is added to many conventional loans with less than 20% down. You can often request removal once you reach 20% equity based on the original value, without refinancing. Ask your servicer first.

**FHA mortgage insurance (MIP)** works differently. On many FHA loans made with less than 10% down, MIP lasts for the life of the loan. If your home's value has grown, refinancing to a conventional loan may drop that monthly cost. Learn more in our [FHA loan guide for Hendersonville](/posts/fha-loan-hendersonville-tn/) and [conventional loan guide](/posts/conventional-loan-hendersonville-tn/).

### Switch From an ARM to a Fixed Rate

If your ARM's fixed period is ending, a fixed-rate refinance can protect you from future rate changes.

### Cash-Out Refinance

A **cash-out refinance** replaces your loan with a larger one and gives you the difference in cash. Fannie Mae generally caps cash-out on a one-unit primary home at 80% of the home's value. Because your whole loan gets the new rate, compare it with a **home equity line of credit (HELOC)**, which leaves your first mortgage alone. Our [Hendersonville HELOC guide](/posts/heloc-hendersonville-tn/) explains the trade-offs.

### Veterans: VA Streamline (IRRRL)

If you have a VA loan, an **Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan (IRRRL)** can lower your rate with less paperwork. See our [VA loan guide for Hendersonville](/posts/va-loan-hendersonville-tn/).

### Shorten Your Term

Moving to a 15-year loan raises the payment but can save a lot of interest. Mortgage News Daily's 15-year fixed index was 6.92% on November 10, 2026.

## Should I Refinance Before the End of the Year?

There's no special deadline, but timing can matter:

- **Holiday schedules** can slow appraisals and closings, so start early if you want to close in December.
- **Don't take on new holiday debt** while your refinance is in process. New credit can change your approval.
- **Rates can change daily.** If the numbers work, ask about locking your rate.
- **Taxes:** Mortgage interest and points have specific IRS rules. Talk to a tax professional about your situation.

4. **Get a Loan Estimate** and compare the APR, closing costs, and break-even point.
