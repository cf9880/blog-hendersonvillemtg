**A conventional loan in Hendersonville TN is a mortgage that isn't backed by a government agency. In 2026, Sumner County's conforming loan limit is $1,029,250 for a one-unit home, well above the $832,750 national baseline. Qualified buyers can put as little as 3% down, and PMI can be removed later.**

Conventional loans are the most common choice for buyers with solid credit. In Sumner County, they also stretch further than in most of the country, because the Nashville area is designated a high-cost market. Here's what local buyers should know this fall.

## Key Takeaways

- **Sumner County's 2026 conforming limit is $1,029,250** for one unit, compared with the $832,750 baseline in most U.S. counties.
- **Loans above $832,750 up to the local limit** are often called "high-balance" conforming loans. Pricing and down payment rules can differ slightly.
- **Down payments start at 3%** for eligible first-time buyers and some income-based programs. Many buyers put 5% to 20% down.
- **PMI is required below 20% down** but, unlike most FHA insurance, it can be removed once you build equity.
- **FHFA usually announces next year's limits in late November.** The 2026 limits were announced November 25, 2025.

## What Is the Conforming Loan Limit in Sumner County?

Conforming loans meet Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac guidelines, including a maximum loan amount set each year by the [Federal Housing Finance Agency](https://www.fhfa.gov/data/conforming-loan-limit). Sumner County is part of the Nashville metro area, which has a higher limit.

| Property type | 2026 Sumner County limit |
|---|---|
| One unit | $1,029,250 |
| Two units | $1,317,650 |
| Three units | $1,592,700 |
| Four units | $1,979,350 |

Loans above these amounts are jumbo loans, which have their own guidelines.

## What Are Conventional Loan Requirements?

Requirements vary by lender and program, but most conventional loans look for:

- **Credit score:** Often 620 or higher. Higher scores usually earn better rates and lower PMI.
- **Debt-to-income ratio:** Commonly up to about 45%, and sometimes up to 50% with strong factors.
- **Down payment:** 3% to 5% minimum for most one-unit primary homes. High-balance loans and multi-unit homes may need more.
- **Reserves and documentation:** Pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, and bank statements.
- **Property use:** Conventional loans can finance primary homes, second homes, and investment properties.

## How Can I Buy With 3% Down?

Two income-based programs allow 3% down: Fannie Mae's [HomeReady](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/originating-underwriting/mortgage-products/homeready-mortgage) and Freddie Mac's [Home Possible](https://sf.freddiemac.com/working-with-us/origination-underwriting/mortgage-products/home-possible). Income limits depend on the property's location. Standard 97% loan-to-value options are also available to many first-time buyers. ## How Does PMI Work and When Does It Go Away?

Private mortgage insurance (PMI) protects the lender when you put less than 20% down. Your cost depends on your credit score, down payment, and loan amount.

Under the Homeowners Protection Act, you can ask to cancel PMI once your balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, if you meet the requirements. It ends automatically at 78%. The [CFPB explains the rules](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/when-can-i-remove-private-mortgage-insurance-pmi-from-my-loan-en-202/). Some lenders also allow removal based on a new appraisal after values rise.

## Conventional, FHA or USDA?

- **Conventional:** Often the lowest total cost with good credit and 5% or more down. PMI can be removed.
- **FHA:** More flexible on credit, but mortgage insurance usually lasts longer.
